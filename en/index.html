An online scam is a crime. And it does not have to go unanswered.

A cybercrime is any crime committed through the internet, a phone or digital systems: fraud, account hacking, extortion, stalking, fake profiles. Scammers hide behind made-up profiles and other people’s accounts, but they always leave traces: numbers, accounts, times and patterns.

A sense of impunity

Many victims give up before they start, out of shame or because they think nothing will happen. Without organized evidence, the case tends to go unanswered and the scammer keeps finding new victims.

Overloaded police stations and few specialists

Public agencies do essential work, but they receive a huge volume of cases and do not always have teams dedicated to cybercrime. Investigations can take time, and online, time erases evidence.

We do not replace the police or the courts. We work alongside them.

How the scam happens

A scam takes minutes. The traces remain.

In the fake relative scam, someone says they changed their number, creates a sense of urgency and asks for a Pix (Brazil’s instant payment). Within minutes the money is gone. But the number, the photo, the account and the times are still there, and that is where we start investigating.

“I’ve been scammed. Now what?”

Breathe. What you do in the next few hours makes a big difference. Follow this list, even if you are not hiring anyone yet.

Do this now

  1. Call your bank immediately. Ask them to block the transaction and, if it was a Pix, to open a MED (Pix Special Refund Mechanism) request. The sooner you act, the better the chances of getting the money back.
  2. Do not delete anything. Conversations, emails, profiles and numbers are evidence, even if it hurts to look at them.
  3. Take screenshots of everything. With the date, time and number or @ visible. Also copy the links to the profiles and ads. If it happened on WhatsApp, export the chat.
  4. Keep the receipts. Pix payments, boletos (payment slips), transfers, statements and card bills.
  5. File a boletim de ocorrência (police report). You can do it online, at your state’s virtual police station.

Don’t

  • Do not pay to release or recover the money. Anyone who charges a fee promising to return the money is usually running a second scam.
  • Do not confront the scammer. They may delete profiles and evidence and disappear.
  • Do not send passwords or codes. No legitimate bank, company or investigator asks for that.
  • Do not blame yourself. Scams are run by professionals to fool anyone. Asking for help quickly is what matters most.
